My $800 iPhone Died, and a 2019 iPod Had to Save It

September 20, 2026

When the expensive device stops being useful

My $800 iPhone died, and suddenly the device I expected to do everything was no longer useful for anything. That is a particularly strange kind of failure: the price suggests reliability, but the result is still a silent, unusable rectangle.

The unexpected backup was a 2019 iPod. It was older, smaller, and never supposed to be the important device in the room. But it still worked, and that was enough to make it useful again.

The experience was a reminder that newer and more expensive do not always mean more dependable. Older technology can stay valuable when it is simple, familiar, and still does the job a person needs.

It also made me think about accessibility. A backup device is only helpful if its controls, feedback, and everyday tasks remain understandable. Keeping working technology around is not just nostalgia; sometimes it is practical resilience.
