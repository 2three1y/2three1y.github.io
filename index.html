Photosensitivity notice

Good news first: nothing on this site flashes or moves, and it stays within the three-flash safety limit.

More details (the boring but important part)
  • What was checked: every animation, transition and script on this page, plus a frame-by-frame screen recording measured against the WCAG 2.3.1 limits (no more than three flashes in any second, including red flashes).
  • Nothing on this site moves or blinks on its own. Not even a little.
  • Your device's Reduce Motion setting is respected: if anything that moves ever gets added, it stays still while that setting is on.
  • This site never plays sound on its own. Ironic, considering the playlist. The only sound here is the startup chime, and it plays only when you press its button.
  • Honest limits: this was tested with automated measurement, not a clinical tool like PEAT, and not on every device.

If anything here ever flickers, that’s a real bug. Please report it on GitHub.

About me

Hey, I’m Carter! You might know me as 2three1y. I go by Carter now, but the username stays. It’s been through too much to retire, and honestly it has seniority.

I poke at tech until it confesses how it works, then turn it into something people can actually use. Sometimes that’s a handy browser tool. Sometimes it’s a quiz that judges your vibes. Both count. Both shipped.

Accessibility goes in at the first line, not duct-taped on at the end. Real headings, keyboard for everything, focus you can see, and stuff that tells you what it’s doing. If a screen reader can’t get around it, it isn’t done. It’s a draft with a domain name.

I’m also a little obsessed with old computer voices, synths and system sounds. Startup chimes, classic speech synths, the tiny noises old hardware makes. If it beeped back in the day, I want it back on speaker.

And I like tools that live on your own device and don’t need anyone’s permission to work. Local-first, decentralized, actually yours. No “please sign in to continue” pop-up ruining the vibe. That’s pretty much what Looscid is about.

Accessibility advocacy

Not optional, not a plugin, not a paid DLC

Accessibility isn’t a feature I add when there’s time left over. It’s the starting line. Semantic HTML, focus you can see, keyboard support for everything, high contrast, structure that makes sense when it’s read out loud, and no important info hiding behind fiddly extra clicks.

If it only works with a mouse, it’s still in early access. If a div is pretending to be a button, it’s catfishing, and I will notice.

Looscid

My local-first workspace (forever in beta, in a good way)

Looscid is an autonomous, local-first, decentralized web workspace built around owning your stuff. Your data and daily tools stay useful right on your own device. It’s still evolving, and no, it’s not trying to replace iOS or Android. It knows its role.

The main quest is screen-reader comfort: predictable landmarks and headings, short chunks, keyboard-first movement, and status updates announced through live regions. The goal is a workspace that stays calm when every single thing is being spoken. Think quiet server at 3 AM, not everyone unmuting in the voice channel at once.
