Photosensitivity notice

Good news first: nothing on this site flashes or moves, and it stays within the three-flash safety limit.

More details (the boring but important part)
  • What was checked: every animation, transition and script on this page, plus a frame-by-frame screen recording measured against the WCAG 2.3.1 limits (no more than three flashes in any second, including red flashes).
  • Nothing on this site moves or blinks on its own. Not even a little.
  • Your device's Reduce Motion setting is respected: if anything that moves ever gets added, it stays still while that setting is on.
  • This site never plays sound on its own. Ironic, considering the playlist.
  • Honest limits: this was tested with automated measurement, not a clinical tool like PEAT, and not on every device.

If anything here ever flickers, that’s a real bug. Please report it on GitHub.

About me

Hey, I’m Carter! You might know me as 2three1y. I go by Carter now, but the username stays. It’s been through too much to retire.

I like poking at tech until I figure out how it works, then turning it into something people can actually use. Sometimes that’s a handy browser tool. Sometimes it’s a quiz that judges your vibes. Both count.

Accessibility is built in from the first line, not bolted on at the end. Real headings, keyboard for everything, focus you can see, and stuff that tells you what it’s doing. If a screen reader can’t get around it, it isn’t done.

I’m also a little obsessed with old computer voices, synths and system sounds. Startup chimes, classic speech synths, the tiny noises old hardware makes. If it beeped back in the day, I want to hear it again.

And I like tools that live on your own device and don’t need anyone’s permission to work. Local-first, decentralized, actually yours. That’s pretty much what Looscid is about.

Accessibility advocacy

Not optional, not a plugin

Accessibility isn’t a feature I add when there’s time left over. It’s the starting line. Semantic HTML, focus you can see, keyboard support for everything, high contrast, structure that makes sense when it’s read out loud, and no important info hiding behind fiddly extra clicks.

If it only works with a mouse, it’s still in early access.

Looscid

My local-first workspace (forever in beta, in a good way)

Looscid is an autonomous, local-first, decentralized web workspace built around owning your stuff. Your data and daily tools stay useful right on your own device. It’s still evolving, and no, it’s not trying to replace iOS or Android.

The main quest is screen-reader comfort: predictable landmarks and headings, short chunks, keyboard-first movement, and status updates announced through live regions. The goal is a workspace that stays calm when every single thing is being spoken.
