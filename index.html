Looscid

My local-first workspace (forever in beta, in a good way)

Looscid is an autonomous, local-first, decentralized web workspace built around owning your stuff. Your data and daily tools stay useful right on your own device. It’s still evolving, and no, it’s not trying to replace iOS or Android. It knows its role.

The main quest is screen-reader comfort: predictable landmarks and headings, short chunks, keyboard-first movement, and status updates announced through live regions. The goal is a workspace that stays calm when every single thing is being spoken. Think quiet server at 3 AM, not everyone unmuting in the voice channel at once.

Lore (About me)

Hey, I’m Chaotic Carter, also known as 2three1y. Chaotic Carter is the name now. 2three1y is the username, and it isn’t going anywhere: it has seniority, it has survived every rename, and it would win the server vote anyway.

My main quest: poke at tech until it confesses how it works, then turn it into something people can actually use. Some days that’s a handy browser tool. Some days it’s a chess game you play by ear. Some days it’s a quiz that judges your vibes. All shipped. All count. No further questions.

Accessibility ships in the first commit, not in a hotfix after launch. Real headings, keyboard for everything, focus you can actually see, and apps that tell you what they’re doing. If a screen reader can’t get around it, it isn’t done. It’s a draft with a domain name.

Side quest that became a main quest: old computer voices, synths and system sounds. Startup chimes, classic speech synths, the tiny beeps old hardware makes. If it beeped back in the day, I want it back on speaker. (You may have already heard mine.)

And I’m into tools that live on your own device and don’t need anyone’s permission to work. Local-first, decentralized, actually yours. No “please sign in to continue” pop-up showing up uninvited. That’s basically what Looscid is about.

Accessibility advocacy

Not optional, not a plugin, not a paid DLC

Accessibility isn’t a feature I add when there’s time left over. It’s the starting line. Semantic HTML, focus you can see, keyboard support for everything, high contrast, structure that makes sense when it’s read out loud, and no important info hiding behind fiddly extra clicks.

If it only works with a mouse, it’s still in early access. If a div is pretending to be a button, it’s catfishing, and I will notice. What the hell, div.

Startup chime (optional, like sleep)

My startup chime is off unless you switch it on. Switch it on and it plays once when the page opens, or on your first tap, click or key press if your browser makes sites wait. It’s soft, about two seconds, and made live in your browser. Whatever you pick stays that way on this device. If your iPhone is on silent, it may stay quiet.

Photosensitivity notice

Good news first: nothing on this site flashes or moves, and it stays within the three-flash safety limit. This is the one part of the site where I keep a straight face (mostly).

More details (the actually important part)
  • What was checked: every animation, transition and script on this page, plus a frame-by-frame screen recording measured against the WCAG 2.3.1 limits (no more than three flashes in any second, including red flashes).
  • Nothing on this site moves or blinks on its own. Not even a little. The most exciting thing that moves here is my bass meter, and it doesn’t.
  • Your device's Reduce Motion setting is respected: if anything that moves ever gets added, it stays still while that setting is on. Your settings outrank me here, as they should.
  • Sound: the only sound here is my startup chime, and it’s off unless you switch it on (the “Startup chime” button in the Startup chime section, or in Settings). When it’s on, it’s soft, about two seconds long, and plays once when the page opens (or on your first tap, click or key press, if your browser makes sites wait). Nothing else on the site makes sound. Ironic, considering the playlist.
  • Honest limits: this was tested with automated measurement, not a clinical tool like PEAT, and not on every device. I have a lot of tabs open, but not that many.

If anything here ever flickers, that’s a real bug, not a feature and not a vibe. Please report it on GitHub and it jumps to the top of my to-do list, ahead of sleep.
